Core Concept in Simple Terms

A singly reinforced beam resists bending moment using concrete in compression (top zone) and longitudinal steel bars in tension (bottom zone). In Limit State Design (LSD), our primary goal is to ensure the cross-section is under-reinforced, meaning steel reaches its yield stress before concrete crushes at its extreme strain limit of 0.0035. This provides noticeable deflections and cracking, giving ample warning before collapse (ductile failure).

Frequent APSC AE Trap Alert

Students frequently substitute overall depth (D) instead of effective depth (d). In IS 456 calculations:

d = D - clear cover - (bar diameter / 2)
APSC Examiner's Bait

"Past paper observation: In APSC AE 2020 (Paper II), Question 42 intentionally supplied an overall depth of 500 mm with 50 mm effective cover. Over 62% of applicants incorrectly multiplied 0.48 × 500 = 240 mm, whereas the accurate answer was 0.48 × 450 = 216 mm."

Quick check: If the calculated depth of neutral axis (xu) is strictly less than the limiting depth (xu,max), the section is classified as:

task_alt Correct! Under-Reinforced Section

When xu < xu,max, steel reaches its design yield stress (0.87 fy) well before concrete attains ultimate crushing strain (0.0035). Tension steel governs, providing secondary moment redistribution and substantial visual warning.
